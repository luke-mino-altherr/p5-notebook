# Love Letters

*After Christopher Strachey, 1952*

## About

In 1952 Christopher Strachey used the Manchester Mark 1, one of the first computers, to write love letters. They were signed "M.U.C." for Manchester University Computer and pinned to the lab noticeboard. They're often called the first computer-generated literature. The word lists and sentence patterns in this sketch are our own, in the same spirit.

## The idea

Writing as a machine. A handful of sentence patterns with blanks, plus lists of words to fill them, produce letters that are different every time, sometimes touching and often absurd.

## The recipe

1. Keep six lists of words: greetings, pet names, describing words, things, "how" words and doing words.
2. Keep a few sentence patterns with blanks, like "YOU ARE MY [describing word] [thing]."
3. Start the letter with a random greeting and pet name.
4. Write three to five sentences. For each one, pick a pattern at random and fill every blank with a random word from the right list.
5. End with "YOURS" and a random "how" word, then sign it "M.U.C."
6. Type the letter out on cream paper one character at a time, in a typewriter font, with a blinking cursor.
7. Show the randomly chosen words in red, so you can see which parts came from the lists.
8. Rest for five seconds, then write a new letter. Clicking starts a new one straight away.

## Building it up

> **1.** Write a sentence on the screen that fills in "YOU ARE MY ___ ___" with a random describing word and a random thing from two lists. *(chance, text)*

> **2.** Add more sentence patterns and word lists, and put several sentences together into a love letter with a greeting and a sign-off. *(rules, repetition)*

> **3.** Lay it out like a typed letter: cream paper, typewriter font, centred, wrapping long lines. *(where, style)*

> **4.** Type it out one character at a time with a blinking cursor, then start a new letter after a pause or when I click. *(change over time, input)*

> **5.** Colour the randomly chosen words red so people can see how it works. *(polish)*

## Make it yours

- Write your own lists: horoscopes, weather reports, recipes, insults, product reviews.
- What if the sentence patterns were lines of a poem, and each line rhymed?
- What if the letter were read aloud, or each word appeared somewhere random on the page?
