# Harmonic Motion

*After John Whitney, 1960s–70s*

## About

John Whitney is often called the father of computer animation. In films like *Permutations* (1968) and *Arabesque* (1975) he moved many points at related speeds, a bit like notes in musical harmony, and filmed the patterns that appeared.

## The idea

Simple motion, many times over. Every dot just goes round in a circle, but because their speeds are related, they keep lining up into spirals, stars and petals, then dissolving again.

## The recipe

1. Fill the screen with a very dark blue.
2. Place 160 dots around the centre. Dot 1 sits closest to the middle and dot 160 furthest out.
3. Every frame, move each dot a little way round its circle. Dot 2 moves twice as fast as dot 1, dot 3 three times as fast, and so on.
4. Let time pass very slowly, so the patterns have time to form.
5. Instead of wiping the screen each frame, cover it with a see-through layer of dark blue, so each dot leaves a short fading trail.
6. Colour the dots like a rainbow from the centre outwards, and slowly cycle the colours.

## Building it up

> **1.** On a dark screen, draw one dot moving round a circle in the middle. *(shapes, change over time)*

> **2.** Make 160 dots, each on its own circle, every circle a bit bigger than the last. *(repetition)*

> **3.** Make each dot faster than the one before: dot 2 twice as fast as dot 1, dot 3 three times as fast, and so on. Slow everything down so I can watch patterns appear. *(rules)*

> **4.** Leave short fading trails behind the dots, and colour them like a rainbow from the centre out, slowly cycling. *(memory, colour)*

## Make it yours

- What if the circles were squashed into ovals, or the dots moved along a flower shape?
- What if each dot played a note when it crossed the top?
- What if the mouse controlled how fast time passes?
