# Schotter

*After Georg Nees, 1968*

## About

Georg Nees was one of the first people to show art made with a computer, in 1965. His drawings were made by a plotter, a machine that moves a pen across paper. *Schotter* means "gravel" in German.

## The idea

Order slowly breaking down. A neat grid of squares crumbles as you read down the page.

## The recipe

1. Make a sheet of cream-coloured paper the size of the window.
2. Imagine a grid 12 squares wide and 22 squares tall, centred on the page.
3. Go through the grid one row at a time, from top to bottom.
4. For each row, work out how messy it should be: not at all for the top row, a little more for each row below.
5. For each square in the row, nudge it sideways and up or down by a random amount, and turn it by a random angle. The messier the row, the bigger the nudge and the turn.
6. Draw the square as a thin black outline.
7. Draw everything once and stop. When someone clicks, roll new random numbers and draw it again.

## Building it up

Each prompt adds one idea. Look at the result before writing the next one.

> **1.** Draw a grid of 12 by 22 squares, outlines only, centred on a cream background. *(shapes, repetition)*

> **2.** Turn each square by a small random angle. *(chance)*

> **3.** Make the randomness grow down the page: the top row perfectly straight, the bottom row very messy. Shift the squares' positions too, not just their angles. *(change)*

> **4.** Draw it once instead of animating, and draw a new version when I click. *(input)*

## Make it yours

- What if the mess grew from the centre outwards instead of top to bottom?
- What if the shapes were circles, letters or tiny drawings?
- What if the mouse position controlled how messy it gets?
