# Substrate

*After Jared Tarbell, 2003*

## About

Jared Tarbell's *Substrate* grows lines that look like cracks in dried mud, or a city seen from above. It was part of a wave of 2000s software art where artists wrote small simulations, let them run, and shaped the result through the rules.

## The idea

Growth. Each crack only knows how to walk straight and stop when it bumps into something, but together they divide the page into a dense, city-like map.

## The recipe

1. Start with pale paper.
2. Sprinkle a few hidden starting points, each pointing in a random direction.
3. Start 12 cracks from them. A crack is a point that walks in a straight line, leaving a thin dark mark.
4. Remember every spot a crack has passed over, and which way it was heading.
5. When a crack walks into another crack or off the edge, it stops. A new crack starts at a random spot on an existing crack and heads off at a right angle.
6. Keep adding cracks until up to 120 are growing at once.
7. For the sand: as each crack moves, look sideways to find how far away the nearest crack is. Scatter a few faint grains of coloured sand in that gap, mostly close to the crack.
8. When someone clicks, start again.

## Building it up

> **1.** Draw a single line that grows slowly across the screen from a random point in a random direction. *(change over time)*

> **2.** When it reaches the edge, start a new line from a random point on the existing line, at a right angle. *(rules)*

> **3.** Grow many lines at once. A line stops when it runs into another line. *(repetition, memory)*

> **4.** Make it feel like cracks in dried mud: thin dark lines on pale paper, up to 120 growing at once. Start again when I click. *(style, input)*

> **5.** Add shading: beside each crack, scatter faint grains of coloured sand into the gap, thicker near the crack. Use earthy colours. *(chance, colour)*

## Make it yours

- What if new cracks branched at 60 degrees instead of 90?
- What if cracks grew from where you click?
- What if each crack were a different colour depending on its direction?
